# Investment Clock + GS throttle: return contrast

**Question.** Does a Goldman-style bull/bear throttle, laid on top of the Merrill Lynch Investment Clock, raise CAGR versus the clock alone?

**Answer.** No. On the production sample with honest CPI/GDP publication lags, no throttle variant raises CAGR by a meaningful amount. The derisk band cuts CAGR by about 3 percentage points. The return-seeking boost, tested on its own, adds **+0.01 percentage points** of CAGR (14.62% vs 14.61%) and lowers Sharpe.

The throttle is implemented and **left off**. `STRATEGY_PARAMS` and the dashboard call sites are unchanged. Nothing here was deployed.

## What the clock already is

The position engine is the four regime weight tables in `config/regime_rules.py` (v7 book: QQQ, SOXX, SPY, IEF, GLD, DBMF, AIPO). Each month is classified the same way as `run_backtest.py`:

- Growth is on if lagged real GDP > 1.5% **or** SPY 12-month momentum > 5%.
- Inflation is on if lagged CPI year-over-year > 3% **and** higher than three months earlier.
- Monthly average VIX > 30 forces Deflation.
- CPI is shifted **+1 month** and GDP **+4 months** before use (FRED dates the period start; the print comes later).

Overlays that already sit on that book (unchanged in every row below): inverse-vol risk parity, HAR volatility target at 13% with cap 1.50, 200-day SPY trend hedge (`bear_equity_frac=0.70`), 7% drawdown breaker, 5 bp transaction costs, 1% borrow spread on gross leverage. Risk-free rate is the mean effective fed funds rate from 2005, 1.87%.

Sample: daily prices **2005-01-04 → 2026-09-21**. Clock mix under the publication lag: goldilocks 70%, reflation 15%, deflation 13%, stagflation 2%.

## GS proxy (not a GS terminal feed)

There is no licensed GS Bull/Bear series in this repo. The series already generated by `scripts/generate_gsblbr.py` (`data/cache/gsblbr_history.csv`) is a public, causal proxy:

Equal-weight average of five expanding-window percentiles (minimum 60 months; each month is ranked only against history up to that month):

| Factor | Series | Sign |
|---|---|---|
| Valuation | S&P 500 / 10-year average price | high = stretched |
| Curve | −(10Y − 2Y), FRED T10Y2Y | high = inverted |
| Inflation | Core CPI year-over-year, CPILFESL | high = hot |
| Labor | −unemployment, UNRATE | high = tight |
| Activity | Industrial production year-over-year, INDPRO | high = strong |

**A high composite is late-cycle bear *risk*, not “the market is falling.”** 2008–09 prints are very low (washout). 2022 prints are very high (overheat; 2022 average 74.8). The backtest may read a month-end print only **one month later** (`release_lag_months=1`), because CPI, unemployment, and industrial production are not published on the observation date.

Bands used for the primary test (same cut-points the dashboard gauge already labels):

| Band | Rule | Share of clock months |
|---|---|---|
| Bull / risk-on | GSBLBR < 45 | 45% |
| Middle | 45 ≤ GSBLBR < 70 | 45% |
| Bear / late-cycle | GSBLBR ≥ 70 | 11% |

## What the throttle does (and does not do)

It does **not** average a second weight table with the clock.

- **Bull.** Mode `derisk`: clock weights unchanged, leverage still allowed. Mode `boost`: only if the clock is already Goldilocks or Reflation, scale the equity sleeve by 1.15 and the vol target by 1.10. A defensive clock stays put.
- **Middle.** Keep the clock’s direction. Multiply the equity sleeve (QQQ, SOXX, SPY, AIPO, …) by 0.75 and park the freed weight in the Deflation book’s non-equity sleeve (IEF / GLD / DBMF). Multiply the vol target by 0.80 and cap the vol scale at 1.0 (no leverage).
- **Bear.** Replace the clock book with the clock’s own Deflation table, vol target ×0.70, vol scale capped at 1.0.

Parameters live in `GS_THROTTLE_PARAMS` (`config/regime_rules.py`). The engine hook is `gs_throttle=` on `run_optimized_regime_backtest`. Default `None` is the production path.

AIPO listed **2025-07-25**. Rows other than `A_prod` and `A_auditor` pass `cash_park_tickers=("AIPO",)`: before the first print, AIPO’s strategic weight earns cash and is not renormalised onto the other ETFs. Other not-yet-listed names (DBMF) are still renormalised, which is the historical engine behaviour.

## Reading the numbers

**Did any variant raise CAGR?** Only `C_boost_only`, and only by **1 basis point** (14.62% vs 14.61%). Max drawdown is 1 bp worse (13.71% vs 13.70%). Sharpe falls from 1.03 to 0.98 because volatility rises from 12.43% to 13.01% and the extra gross exposure is financed. That is not a raise.

**Derisk does what derisk does.** B, B_wide, and B_mild cut CAGR by **2.7 to 3.1 percentage points** and improve max drawdown by **0.8 to 0.9 percentage points**. Sharpe does not improve (0.98–0.99 vs 1.03): return falls at least as much as risk. The clock’s max drawdown is already 13.7%, inside the 14.8% limit, so the drawdown the throttle buys is not a binding constraint.

**Stacking the boost on the derisk band does not earn the CAGR back.** C is 11.60%, still 3.0 points behind A, and it gives the drawdown improvement back (max drawdown 13.69%, in line with A) with a worse Sharpe (0.92). C_hot’s max drawdown **worsens** vs A, by 0.19 points, and CAGR is still 2.9 points lower.

**Why the boost cannot rescue the derisk.** The bull band is 45% of months and the middle band is another 45%. Cutting equity and killing leverage on nearly half the sample, including every ordinary expansion that is merely “not a trough,” dominates any 10–15% vol-target bump in the bull band. Widening or softening the band (B_wide, B_mild) does not change the sign.

**Why a pure boost also fails.** GSBLBR low means “conditions are not stretched,” which includes the washout *after* a break, not a clean risk-on flag. In 2008 the composite peaked at 36.8, so the whole year is the bull band. The bear override never fires in the crash it would most like to catch; the existing VIX>30 rule, 200-day filter, and drawdown breaker are what keep 2008 positive. Raising the vol target in that same bull band adds risk the sample does not pay for. The late-cycle override does help 2022 (−12.0% → −9.9%), which is real and too small to fund a 3-point CAGR cut.

## Reconciling 14.85% and 12.21%

On this sample the two figures are different code paths, not two estimates of the same path:

- **14.85%** is `A_prod`: the production engine (missing ETFs renormalised) with CPI +1 month and GDP +4 months. It matches the live print that had been described as unlagged. Turning the publication lag **off** is a different number: **15.52%** CAGR and a worse max drawdown (14.48%).
- **12.21%** is `A_auditor`: that same publication-lagged regime, shifted one further month. That is what `run_dashboard.py` stores as the auditor’s “lagged” result (`regime.shift(1)` on an already lagged history). It is a harsher delay than the release calendar, and it is not the production baseline used for A/B/C.

Labels agree on 85% of months between the lagged and unlagged clocks (38 months differ). The comparison above uses the publication lag only.

Holding AIPO as cash instead of renormalising it costs **0.24 points** of CAGR (14.85% → 14.61%) and improves max drawdown by 0.20 points. B and C are judged against A, so that cash drag is not counted as a throttle effect.

## Recommendation

Keep the Investment Clock as the only position engine. Do not turn the GS throttle on for return-seeking, and do not turn the derisk band on for drawdown: the drawdown gain is under 1 point and the CAGR cost is about 3 points, with no Sharpe benefit.

The proxy is still a reasonable **display** cross-check (it was high through 2022 and low in the 2009 trough). It is a poor **position** throttle, because a high reading means “late cycle” and a low reading means “already washed out,” which is the wrong shape for a coincident risk switch.

Reproduce with `python3 ab_gs_throttle.py`. Raw rows are in `out/gs_throttle_results.csv`.
